afdEP-comm: DeepEP V2 specialized for AFD roles

Baseline: FastAFD's DeepEP M2N, the way FastAFD runs AFD over DeepEP V2 (DeepEPM2NAdapter): one symmetric expert-parallel group, dummy expert slots on Attention ranks, every rank sending, receiving and waiting in both phases, upstream combine. The benchmarks run it over the same vendored kernels (benchmarks/fastafd.py); tables below call it FastAFD M2N. afdEP-comm's changes apply in one NVLink domain and across NVLink domains joined by GIN, and the baseline's unspecialized instantiations keep the upstream template arguments and kernels. Records written before 1.1.0 call the baseline "Masked" or "Masked DeepEP" and its arm role_aware=False, and afdEP-comm "the role path"; 1.1.0 removed that flag.

Digests: native source digests in the records are those of the trees as measured. 958652a3 is the tree of commit 32c871d, which merged the directional protocol; the commits after it changed only comments, notices and other text in the native tree (the 1.0.0 release tree is 131efc37). Older digests name the trees each section measured; the 4 × H100 result below, for example, is bd4db4da, which predates sections 10 and 13–16. The earlier DeepEP-reuse role specialization, v0, has its own record (validation). Sections 17 and 18 were measured on development trees; their published commits differ only in comments and text: 200944ce, 2d22e5e9, 7b8ed8b5 and 81b71eb0 are published as adfbaf65, b3821109, ede73b32 and c065385b, and the comment fixes after review give 34fa5eac. The rename tree, 8e63d39f, differs from section 19's b5078d62 only in license and provenance files (LICENSE.FastAFD and the two hash records). The 1.1.0 tree, 21298d5b, differs from it only in section 17's host-side switch functions, which no longer read the environment, and in comments and the hash records; no kernel changed.

afdEP-comm ships a change only if it depends on the AFD roles. A change that would speed up FastAFD's DeepEP M2N as well is a DeepEP improvement, not an AFD one, and is rejected here even when it is faster (R1–R3). Every shipped change keeps a measurement control switch, except section 17 from 1.1.0: the sparse directional hybrid is the directional protocol itself, measured against the dense protocol of 1.0.0 as its own tree (value and evidence).

1. Entry barriers​

Upstream dispatch and combine each open with an all-rank NVLink barrier plus a grid sync, so every call waits for the slowest peer to arrive before writing. On afdEP-comm both are implied by order (proof beside afdep_skip_entry_barriers in csrc/kernels/elastic/afdep_roles.hpp): each rank runs complete dispatch→combine pairs on one ordered stream, the main kernels launch without PDL after their epilogues, and every exchange-(N+1) remote write follows an exit barrier that each peer reaches only after its exchange-N inbox reads. All afdEP-comm's ranks skip both barriers; exit barriers, credits, counters and lifetimes are unchanged. AFDEP_KEEP_ENTRY_BARRIERS=1 restores them as a measurement control.

AFD basis: the proof needs the role order (sources never receive dispatch payloads, FFN ranks never receive combine payloads); a symmetric world, where every rank writes every peer's inbox in both phases, keeps the barrier. On the clean build the skip saves 7.3 µs per one-token round trip at H2048 (30.1 → 22.8 µs) and 6.8 µs at H4096 (development screens: validation-barriers.json).

The GPU matrix passes with barriers skipped, including delayed FFN replies, a delayed Attention source before dispatch, changing graph replays, host-count reuse and invalid-route assertions.

2. Directional count exchange​

Upstream dispatch reduces the union count vector (every rank and logical expert) across all transport CTAs on every rank, sends it to every peer and waits for every peer's vector, so Attention ranks wait for always-zero counts and FFN ranks reduce their own zero counts. In afdEP-comm Attention sources reduce and send only FFN rank/expert counts to FFN ranks and wait for none; FFN ranks skip their own reduction and wait only for source counts. Prefix sums and host counts keep their canonical positions (sources publish zeros). AFDEP_UNION_NOTIFY=1 restores the union exchange as a measurement control.

AFD basis: dispatch counts flow one way because sources receive no tokens and FFN ranks send none; a symmetric rank both sends and receives.

Development screens: validation-notify.json.

3. Directed exit​

Upstream ends dispatch and combine with an all-rank barrier, so a sender also waits for every receiver and every rank waits for unrelated peers. On the role path, senders (Attention in dispatch, FFN in combine) flush their TMA stores, grid-sync and release a per-peer monotonic arrival counter in each receiver's workspace (the direct path's unused scale-out channel tails); receivers wait on SM0 for every sender's counter to reach their own round. The section 1 ordering argument carries over: exchange-(N+1) writes into FFN inboxes follow the Attention rank's receipt of the FFN's combine-N arrival, which the FFN releases after its copy epilogue and counter clears; writes into Attention inboxes follow the FFN's receipt of the Attention rank's dispatch arrival, which follows its reduce epilogue. AFDEP_ALL_RANK_EXIT=1 restores the exit barriers as a measurement control.

AFD basis: each phase has one sending role and one receiving role, so a sender has nothing to wait for; in a symmetric world every rank is both.

Development screens and GPU matrix: validation-exit.json.

Per-CTA release: the sending role receives nothing in its phase, so it does not need the grid sync before releasing its arrival. Each sending CTA flushes its own TMA stores and releases its own arrival; receivers wait for round × sender CTAs (every rank uses the same transport SM count) and the last source CTA to finish resets the slot counters. AFDEP_GRID_SYNC_EXIT=1 keeps the grid-synced release as a control; against it the control is slower in every cell and repeat (H2048 random: +9.7% at one token, 22.68 vs 20.74 µs, to +0.9% at 2,048; H4096 +7.1% to +0.4%). With it the one-token round trip is 20.7 µs at H2048 (FastAFD M2N 31.6) and 28.4 µs at H4096 (FastAFD M2N 38.6) (record).

4. Role-sized registered buffers​

AFD basis: in one NVLink domain only Attention sources write FFN dispatch inboxes and only FFN ranks write Attention combine inboxes. Union sizing reserves a dispatch region for every rank (FFN ranks never send) and, in the rank layout, a combine region for every rank (Attention ranks never reply). afdEP-comm sizes dispatch for the N sources (source s writes region s - F; the copy epilogue reads with the same offset) and combine for the F FFN senders. Every rank registers the same maximum, as the symmetric window requires. Without the rank layout (more ranks than top-k) combine keeps its top-k regions.

7. Directional hybrid protocol​

AFD basis: DeepEP's hybrid (multi-domain) protocol is union-shaped. Every rank exchanges counts with every scale-out peer, sends finish tails to all of them and forwards for all of them, and both entry barriers include a cross-domain RDMA barrier, so an Attention domain waits on an FFN domain's zero counts and tails and vice versa. When every NVLink domain holds one role (FFN ranks divide into whole domains), dispatch traffic flows Attention → FFN only and combine traffic FFN → Attention only:

  • Attention domains send counts, payloads and finish tails to FFN domains and skip count reception, forwarding and combine reduction.

  • FFN domains skip scale-out sends, wait only for Attention domains, and signal combine arrival to Attention domains only.

  • Both hybrid entry barriers keep only their scale-up (intra-domain) half. Every exchange-(N+1) cross-domain write follows its reader's exchange-N consumption through the round trip (argument beside afdep_directional_hybrid in csrc/kernels/elastic/afdep_roles.hpp).

  • In this mode an Attention domain shares no state (no peer writes its tails, counters or slot allocators), so Attention domains also skip their scale-up barriers: −2.6% at one token (74.80 → 72.87 µs) to −0.4% at 2,048 against the protocol without the skip, every size faster in all repeats (record).

Correction (section 11): as first shipped, this protocol let RDMA staging overlap inboxes another domain writes. That was a race in layouts with two or more FFN domains or two or more Attention domains. It is fixed, and those layouts pay 1–2.7% at decode sizes for the fix. The multi-domain latency records below predate the fix.

Layouts that mix roles in a domain keep the union hybrid protocol; AFDEP_UNION_HYBRID=1 keeps it as a control. A per-phase globaltimer trace of the union protocol at one token showed the cross-domain entry barriers alone on the critical path for about 17 µs of the 88 µs round trip.

10. Single-FFN reduction​

AFD basis: DeepEP's FFN combine reduces each token's local top-k rows with a branch-free prefix of ceil(top_k / ranks) slots. That is an expected local row count for tokens spread over every rank, and in afdEP-comm it counts the Attention ranks, which hold no experts. With one FFN rank, every token's top-k rows are local, so all top_k slots are valid on every token and the whole reduction takes the branch-free path. AFDEP_UNION_REDUCE_PREFIX=1 keeps the union prefix as a control.

Two or more FFN ranks keep the upstream prefix. There a larger prefix is generic tuning: in a symmetric 4-rank world it is also faster (up to −7.5%). At 2A2F H4096 the role-sized prefix was 2–10% slower.

11. Staging outside remotely written inboxes (fix)​

Section 7 dropped the cross-domain entry barriers. Those barriers had also ordered when staging buffers could be reused, and the dropped part was not covered by the section 7 argument. That argument covers readers consuming inboxes, not a sender's NIC still reading its own staging:

  • Attention side. An Attention domain's dispatch staging overlapped its combine scale-out inbox. FFN domain 0 can write its replies there as soon as its own dispatch arrives, while this NIC is still reading staging for FFN domain 1.
  • FFN side. An FFN domain's combine staging overlapped its dispatch scale-out inbox. Attention domain 1 can write its next dispatch there as soon as its own replies arrive, while this NIC is still reading staging for Attention domain 2.

The race shows up when one destination lags. Fault injection that defers one destination's puts reproduces both cases (patches in the record):

  • the second Attention domain's replies mismatched 6.1% of elements;
  • the second FFN domain hit an illegal address from corrupted tokens.

With one domain on the other side, every reply or next request follows that domain's per-channel signals, each sent after the channel's puts on the same QP, so those layouts are safe and unchanged. The fix:

  • with two or more FFN domains, Attention domains stage at window offset 0, their scale-up inboxes, which are unused in both phases in this mode;
  • with two or more Attention domains, FFN domains stage in a slice that calculate_buffer_size reserves at the window end.

Results (record):

  • Both injected cases pass after the fix.
  • The 14 two-node cases, ten three-pod cases (two FFN or two Attention domains, BF16/FP8, small/large, generic) and the single-domain matrix pass on native digest a32a12d1.
  • The two-node layout is unchanged (within ±0.4%).
  • The three-pod layouts pay 1–2.7% up to 512 tokens and about 0.1% at 2,048.

12. Direct GIN path: in-kernel route checks​

With hybrid off across NVLink domains (direct RDMA to every rank), afdEP-comm used to run FastAFD M2N's union kernels plus the route-guard kernel. Its dispatch now instantiates the role template with kAfdFfnRanks = F, so routes are checked in-kernel as in sections 8 and 1. The barrier, count and exit protocol is still union.

Against the guard-kernel control the change is −1.0% to −1.6% at every size up to 512 tokens across two nodes, and it brings the path level with FastAFD M2N. Direct BF16/FP8 small and large cases and all eight invalid-route cases pass (record).

Why this path matters at decode sizes: FastAFD M2N with hybrid off is within 4% of hybrid on at 1–32 tokens across two nodes. With two Attention domains it ties or beats afdEP-comm's directional hybrid at 8 tokens (97.3 vs 99.1 µs). So the one-hop direct path is the base for porting sections 1–3 to RDMA.

Known limitation (pre-existing). With one or three FFN ranks on 2-GPU pods, an FFN and an Attention rank share a node, and direct mode fails for FastAFD M2N as well as for afdEP-comm. The likely cause is DeepEP's direct GIN mode mixing same-node NVLink stores with RDMA signals. Direct-mode work is therefore limited to role-pure nodes.

13. Single-FFN collect view​

AFD basis: with one FFN rank, each Attention token has exactly one reply row, the FFN's final sum. Only that FFN writes the Attention combine inbox, and only after the source's next dispatch. So the reduce epilogue is a pure copy.

The source dispatch zeroes the rows of tokens with no valid route, which no FFN writes. Layouts with more than one FFN rank, and calls with weights or bias, return the ordinary result.

14. Role suites (launch only what each role uses)​

In afdEP-comm an Attention rank runs {dispatch send, combine receive} and an FFN rank {dispatch receive, combine send}, but the shared path launched the same kernels, in the same shape, on both roles. FastAFD M2N ranks send and receive in every phase, so every warp class works there; in afdEP-comm some classes never do.

This is the idle-warp anomaly. In the shared directional hybrid launches (1F:1A, native 8205d02b, one token), both roles launched the dispatch as 24 blocks × 640 threads and the combine as 24 × 512, each block at maximum shared memory. 16. Count-first dispatch with a streaming FFN expansion (opt-in, one NVLink domain)​

AFD basis: in one NVLink domain the FFN only receives in dispatch, so its idle SMs could expand rows into expert slots while the sources are still pushing. A FastAFD M2N FFN also sends and must wait for its own sends. The 3:1 analysis below showed why the expansion cannot start early today: it needs the per-expert prefix sums, and the counts behind them arrive with the end of the payload on the same link.

With AFDEP_COUNT_FIRST_MIN_BUCKET=b, buckets of at least b tokens run three role kernels (generated copies, tests/role_kernels.py, plus afdep_count_first.cuh):

  • Sources (afdep_source_dispatch.cuh): every notify thread counts tokens (one round instead of up to 22 at 2,048 tokens); the count word carries a per-source epoch in its high half; each stored row releases an epoch-valued flag in the FFN's workspace. By default payload warps also wait until the counts are issued (a gate); AFDEP_COUNT_FIRST_UNGATE=1 removes it, which measures better (below): fast counting alone gets the counts out before much payload is queued, so the transfer need not wait.
  • FFN receive (afdep_ffn_stream_dispatch.cuh): triggers the epilogue once every count has arrived and publishes the prefix sums through a release word.
  • Epilogue (afdep_ffn_stream_epilogue.cuh): blocks claim units of rows, chunk-major and source-minor, wait for each row's flag and expand it; the last claimant waits for the dispatch grid and clears the two cleared words. Flags are epoch-valued and never cleared.

The words live in the scale-out signal-tail region that one NVLink domain does not use. Below b, and by default (b = 0), every kernel is the existing one. Count-first is visible on the wire, so every rank must use the same configuration; a mismatch is rejected at construction on every rank. AFDEP_PAYLOAD_FIRST=1 forces the existing kernels.

17. Sparse directional hybrid (default across NVLink domains)​

AFD basis: with one FFN NVLink domain and S Attention domains (directional hybrid), the per-channel signals scale with the channel count C, not with the tokens. Every source sends a finish tail on all C channels (S·C RDMA atomics into each FFN NIC: 2,112 at 11:1 with 24 SMs), the FFN signals every source on every channel in combine (another S·C, out of the same NIC), and token i of every source rides channel i mod C, so at small batches all S first tokens queue on FFN forward warp 0. FastAFD's DeepEP M2N has the same signals, but there every domain sends and receives in both phases. Here the flow is one-directional, so they converge on the few FFN NICs, and each source knows exactly which channels it used. Three parts, each through kernels generated by tests/role_kernels.py (afdep_sparse_hybrid.cuh holds the words); shared kernels are unchanged. From 1.1.0 all three always run in the directional hybrid. Until then each was an opt-in switch (AFDEP_HYBRID_STAGGER, AFDEP_HYBRID_SPARSE_DISPATCH, AFDEP_HYBRID_SPARSE_COMBINE, with the stagger bound AFDEP_HYBRID_STAGGER_MAX_TOKENS), and the measurements below were taken with them; 1.1.0 removes them, and the kernels are the ones measured with all three set and no bound.

  • Stagger: source s puts token i on channel (i + s·C/S) mod C, so the sources' first tokens use different FFN forward warps, SMs and QPs. A channel still carries at most ceil(T/C) tokens of a source.
  • Sparse dispatch: SM 0 of each source writes its token count + 1 to a word per source domain on every FFN rail peer at entry, and a channel that carried no token sends no finish tail. FFN forwarders retire such a lane once the count shows the channel unused (data never waits on the count); after the exit barrier's grid sync, SM 0 waits for every count and clears it. An FFN NIC then takes S·min(T, C) finish atomics plus S count words instead of S·C.
  • Sparse combine: each source channel records in a local word the FFN domains it put a token for. The FFN combine signals a source on a channel only if it replied to that source there (it received the source's tokens there in dispatch, and replies to every token it received), plus channel 0 to every source. The Attention collect waits for exactly those signals.

Why the protocol holds. Each awaited signal still follows that channel's puts on the same QP, so every reply a source waits for has landed, and the source waits on exactly the channels the FFN replies on. Channel 0 is a done witness: every combine signal follows the FFN's dispatch kernel in stream order, so even a source that sent nothing cannot start its next exchange (count word, tails, inbox writes) before the FFN has consumed and cleared this one. Count words are cleared in that dispatch kernel, before any combine signal. The witness must also certify the FFN combine's own reads: a rail forwarder's combine scale-up inbox (the holders' partials) can overlap the dispatch scale-out inbox a source writes next (at FP8, once a forwarder has passed a few thousand tokens to one holder), and a source waits only for its own channels and channel 0. So every forward warp releases a rank-local DONE count after its last read, and channel 0 sends the witness only after all C have. An earlier tree (2d22e5e9) sent the witness after channel 0's own warp only; an independent review found the gap (see below). Staging is unaffected: unused channels stage nothing, and every used channel's staging reads are covered by the same finish tails and signals as before (with two or more Attention domains, FFN replies stage in the reserved slice of section 11). The parts change the wire protocol, so every rank must resolve them alike; afdep/deepep.py gathers each rank's resolved values after construction and rejects a mismatch on every rank (for example an FFN-only AFDEP_SHARED_HYBRID_FORWARD=1, which turns sparse dispatch off on the FFN ranks only and would otherwise hang); the same tuple carries the mixed-role F (section 18) and the FP8 combine replies setting and its minimum bucket (section 19). Other topologies, one NVLink domain included, report the parts off, so they never reject there. The parts need the role-suite shapes: AFDEP_SHARED_ROLE_KERNELS=1 or an AFDEP_SHARED_HYBRID_* control set on every rank turns the parts that need those shapes off (these ablation controls change the kernel shapes too, so they are not a clean control for the sparse protocol), and the check rejects such a control set on one role only. The check runs once, at construction, while the kernels read those controls at every launch: set them before constructing the Buffer and do not change them afterwards.

18. Mixed-role directional hybrid (opt-in, FFN ranks sharing an NVLink domain)​

AFD basis: at high Attention:FFN ratios the FFN group is smaller than a node, so on 8-GPU (or 4-GPU) nodes the FFN ranks share their NVLink domain with Attention ranks. The directional protocol (section 7) needs one role per domain, so these layouts fall back to the union hybrid, which matches FastAFD M2N within 1.4%: AFD buys nothing there. Measured on kermit (6 pods × 4 H200, FP8), at 11:1 the union path takes 211.8 µs at one token, and its small-batch cost is synchronisation, not data: cutting the transport from 24 to 8 SMs saves 17% at one token but costs 56% at 512, and a per-kernel trace shows every GPU, pure-Attention ones included, spending about 85-115 µs in dispatch at both 1 and 32 tokens. Splitting nodes into single-role domains (section 17's layout) recovers small batches but funnels the FFN's ingress through its own few NICs (1.7× slower than the mixed layout at 128 tokens on 3 × 8 GPUs).

  • every domain-0 GPU stays its rail's forwarder, so remote tokens still enter through all U NICs and are forwarded over NVLink;
  • only ranks below F hold experts (the in-kernel route bound is F × experts per rank);
  • domain 0's Attention GPUs (A0) are also sources: their own tokens take the existing local bypass into their own forwarder (rail lane 0), they count to themselves, and their own replies are written locally by their forward warps;
  • pure-Attention domains run the directional Attention suite (send block, one-CTA collect, no barriers), and the rail halves of every barrier are gone.

19. FP8 combine replies (opt-in, across NVLink domains)​

AFD basis: in the directional hybrid every reply byte of S Attention domains leaves through the few NICs of the FFN domain (each FFN NIC sends S_A·T reply rows while each Attention NIC receives F_D·T). Motivating measurements on kermit (6 × 4 H200, InfiniBand, 4 FFN + 20 Attention ranks, Qwen3-235B shapes, hidden 4096, cross-node tree 34fa5eac; the same layout's screens with this switch are in the record): above about 64 tokens per Attention GPU the round trip is bound by bytes through the FFN node's NICs at 0.33–0.45 µs per arriving token; a token moves about 4.2 KB of FP8 dispatch (with scales) and 8.2 KB of BF16 combine replies, so replies are two thirds of the bytes, and the FFN combine kernel takes about 81 µs of a 179 µs round trip at 64 tokens.

With AFDEP_HYBRID_FP8_COMBINE=1 (every rank alike, set before constructing the Buffer), for every exchange whose bucket is at least AFDEP_HYBRID_FP8_COMBINE_MIN_BUCKET tokens (default 0, every bucket; latched and checked with the switch; smaller buckets keep the BF16 path unchanged, and since every rank passes the same bucket both roles pick the same format):

  • An FFN forward warp reduces each reply row exactly as before (the same loads, BF16 adds and FP32 accumulation, so the same BF16 row), then quantizes it in registers to E4M3 with one power-of-two scale 2^e per 128 elements, e the smallest integer with amax ≤ 448 · 2^e (exact from amax's FP32 bits, clamped at −117), and stores it in the first part of the same shared-memory TMA row. One TMA store stages it and one put sends H + align32(H/128) bytes into the same (slot, token) row of the Attention inbox: 4,128 B instead of 8,256 B at H = 4096 (−50.0%; −49.8% at 7168 and 8192).
  • Row format: E4M3 values at [0, H), then byte g = 127 + e_g, so groups 4p..4p+3 form DeepEP's UE8M0 sf_pack_t p; no metadata (expanded layout, no top-k weights, asserted on both sides). UE8M0 rather than FP32 scales: scaling and dequantizing are exact in FP32 (every nonzero product stays normal), so the only rounding is the E4M3 cast and the GPU suite can check the result bit-exactly; it is also 96 B per row smaller.
  • The Attention reduce epilogue (a generated copy of combine_reduce_epilogue.cuh, same launch shape and slot deduplication) dequantizes each FFN domain's row, sums the rows and any bias in FP32 in slot order (ascending last top-k lane per domain), and rounds once to BF16.
  • Unchanged: the scale-up (NVLink) partials stay BF16, and so do both collects, every protocol word, signal and DONE witness, channels, QPs, launch shapes, shared memory, window sizing and every shared kernel's bytes, code string and cache key. The kernels are afdep_ffn_fp8_hybrid_combine.cuh (afdep_ffn_sparse_fp8_hybrid_combine.cuh under sparse combine), afdep_fp8_combine_reduce.cuh and afdep_fp8_combine_reduce_epilogue.cuh, generated by tests/role_kernels.py, plus afdep_fp8_combine.cuh.

Why the protocol holds. The format is a function of the bucket, which every rank passes identically per exchange, so a format change between back-to-back exchanges is always a bucket change, which already moves every region; each exchange's writer and reader use the same format, signals are words rather than byte counts, and the FFN rewrites each row's scale padding, so nothing carries a row stride from one exchange to the next. Both FP8 regions keep their BF16 base addresses: the reply inbox still starts at the end of the scale-up buffer, and the FFN staging at the BF16 inbox's end (one Attention domain) or at the reserved window-end slice (two or more). Every FP8 row is smaller than the BF16 row, so every FP8 byte lies inside the BF16 region it replaces, and the staging and ordering arguments of sections 7, 11 and 17 apply unchanged: no word, slot, signal, message count or ordering changes. FFN and Attention compute the same (slot, token) addresses from the same base, region count and bucket.

Switch and agreement. The row format is on the wire, and a mismatch would mis-decode silently instead of hanging, so the native ElasticBuffer latches the switch at construction (setting it later has no effect) and reports it, with its minimum bucket, as the fifth and sixth elements of the tuple section 17 checks; afdep/deepep.py rejects a mismatch on every rank and exposes the values as Buffer.fp8_combine and Buffer.fp8_combine_min_bucket. The minimum defaults to 0, every bucket; negative values clamp to 0, so neither turns FP8 off, and it reports 0 whenever FP8 is off. It is on only in the directional hybrid: one NVLink domain, mixed-role domains (section 18), AFDEP_UNION_HYBRID=1, the baseline's buffers, deterministic mode and builds without SM90 features report it off, and AFDEP_SHARED_ROLE_KERNELS=1 turns it off (set on one role only, the check rejects it). Each combine asserts it still runs the directional hybrid with the expanded, unweighted layout. It composes with the stagger and sparse dispatch (dispatch-only), with sparse combine (the FFN runs the sparse FP8 copy), with both collects and with AFDEP_SHARED_HYBRID_COLLECT=1 (neither touches reply data), and with BF16 or FP8 dispatch.

Accuracy. Each FFN domain's BF16 partial is rounded to E4M3 before the FP32 sum: per element the error is at most 2^-4 of its 128-group's amax (relative 2^-4 in the normal range, roughly 2.5–3% RMS), elements below roughly 2^-14 to 2^-15 of the amax become subnormal and below roughly 2^-18 to 2^-19 flush to zero. NaN stays NaN at its own element; an infinity sets its group's scale to 2^120, so it decodes to infinity and the group's other elements below about 2^110 become zero. The same top scale means a finite group amax of 248·2^120 (about 3.3e38) or more also decodes to infinity: the top binade of BF16 is not representable. DeepSeek-V3 keeps its combine in BF16; this switch needs a model-level accuracy check (per-layer MoE-output error, next-token KL and greedy match, a small task suite) before any serving use, and communication-only evidence supports no quality claim. The GSM8K check under Status is the task-level part; the per-layer error and next-token KL are still open.

Expected gain, not measured: half the scale-out reply bytes at the same message and signal counts, so the byte-bound part of the round trip at 64 tokens and above should shrink by up to a third (the dispatch bytes stay); at 16 tokens or fewer, where latency and signals dominate, about neutral. One NVLink domain, mixed-role and union paths are unchanged by construction.

The time is again on the FFN side: at 512 tokens an Attention rank's sends are issued in 24 µs, but the FFN domain's dispatch (RDMA receive and NVLink forwarding) takes 312 µs, its expansion 30 µs and its combine 148 µs, while the Attention's one-CTA collect waits 461 µs. Each FFN GPU receives the three Attention GPUs on its rail, 1,536 tokens (6.3 MB) in 312 µs: about 20 GB/s of a 50 GB/s port, so not line rate. It is not channel parallelism either: with 16, 24, 32 and 48 transport SMs the 512-token round trip is 479, 493, 463 and 483 µs (more channels only add latency at one token, 100 → 143 µs). Nor is it the three-into-one fan-in: at 1:1 (4F:4A) an FFN GPU's dispatch takes 135.6 µs for 512 tokens and 289–290 µs for 1,536, against 312 µs for the same 1,536 tokens from three sources. The limit is the FFN GPU's own receive-and-forward rate, about 21 GB/s here whatever the fan-in, and 3:1 triples the tokens each FFN GPU carries. That rate belongs to the transport and applies to FastAFD M2N as well.

Direct pays little per source but is slower than hybrid from 32 tokens (131 against 108 µs with two domains, 761 against 351 at 512), so it is no 3:1 answer. The hybrid cost grows with channels × sources: at one token the latency slope per added channel (64 to 384 channels) is 0.061 µs with one source, 0.093 with two and 0.168 with three, and the two-source gap extrapolates to about 7 µs at zero channels. In the code every channel sends a finish atomic to each FFN domain in dispatch, and the FFN sends a signal per channel to each Attention domain in combine: 2 × channels × sources atomics through each FFN GPU's NIC per round trip (1,152 at 192 channels and three sources), all but 2 × sources of them from channels that carry no data at one token. Every source's first token also rides channel 0, so the FFN forwards and reduces them one after another (the zero-channel term). Splitting each channel's sources across a second set of FFN warps cannot help: the waits on sources already run in parallel, one lane each, and an empty source costs one loop iteration.

Removing the signals needs the FFN to know which channels carry data before it waits, which is count-first dispatch with per-channel occupancy: a wire-protocol change, AFD-specific (counts flow one way; the FFN never sends in dispatch), modelled at about −32 µs at 4F:12A and one token, and the same change the one-domain expansion overlap needs. It is not part of the role suites, which keep the shipped protocol; section 17 implements it across NVLink domains (the sparse protocol). Fewer channels are a protocol-neutral setting (16 instead of 24 SMs saved 9 µs at 4F:12A and one token, flat at 512) but help FastAFD M2N as well.

Parked​

Relay dispatch, for layouts with more FFN than Attention ranks, is validated and kept on an unpublished branch. Its 1A:3F results: −6% at 128 tokens and −22% at 2,048. It is neutral or slower in a symmetric EP world. It is parked because the AFD plugin's layouts all have N ≥ F.

Rejected​

R1. FFN combine sizing and unroll 2 (generic)​

The FFN combine reduction's branch-free top-k prefix is ceil(top_k / ranks), with dummy Attention ranks counted, and it unrolls by four. An AFD prefix of ceil(top_k / ffn_ranks) spills at unroll 4, so it shipped with unroll 2. The audit applied unroll 2 to FastAFD M2N's combine: FastAFD M2N alone gained up to 20% (H4096), and the AFD prefix over upstream's was mixed (better at H2048 512–2,048 tokens, worse at H4096 8–128). The change is removed.

R2. Few-token split combine (generic)​

Spreading a few tokens' hidden chunks across idle combine warps cut FFN combine latency, but the same split applied to FastAFD M2N gained up to 32% (H4096, 32 tokens): upstream's one-warp-per-token loop is slow for both. Removed.

R3. Transport grid per bucket (generic)​

A full-device transport grid for buckets of at least 512 tokens was 10–16 points faster than the 24/132 default, but the same knob speeds up FastAFD M2N equally: it is a generic DeepEP launch setting, not an AFD property, so afdEP-comm does not ship it. The screens remain in the record. Sections 4–7 below were measured while that policy was active: their paired changes compare arms that shared it, but their absolute 512- and 2,048-token values include it.

R5. Attention receive merge (no meaningful gain)​

AFD basis: Attention ranks send nothing in combine, so their main combine kernel only waits for FFN arrivals before the PDL reduce epilogue. A scratch build skipped it and let every reduce-epilogue CTA wait on the arrival counters. Paired against the two-kernel path (H2048, three repeats) it changed one-token latency by −1.3% (22.67 → 22.45 µs) and other sizes by −1.8% to +0.4%, while occupying every Attention SM for the whole wait instead of one. Not shipped (record).

R6. Voided screen of the per-CTA exit​

The first screen of section 3's per-CTA release selected the tree with PYTHONPATH while python -m put the HEAD tree first on sys.path, so both arms ran HEAD (void record). Screens now isolate trees with PYTHONSAFEPATH=1 and record each arm's native digest; the re-run shipped the change.

R7. Hybrid role-sized buffers (latency cost)​

AFD basis: with the directional protocol each role uses only part of the hybrid window, so the inboxes can be sized for the Attention source domains (dispatch scale-up/scale-out, combine scale-up and staging) and the FFN domains (combine scale-out). Across two nodes this halved registered transport memory (78.8 → 40.0 MB at capacity 2,048, H2048) and passed every cross-node and single-domain case, but the union-buffer control was faster in every repeat up to 512 tokens (one token: 73.07 vs 74.10 µs, −1.4%). Latency is the primary objective and the saving is about 39 MB per rank at this capacity, so it is not shipped (record).

R8. Per-CTA counts (slower)​

AFD basis: sources never consume counts, and with per-CTA exit every source CTA already releases its own arrival, so its partial counts could travel with it instead of through SM0's grid-wide reduction and put (a one-token trace showed SM0 releasing at 2.1 µs, the other CTAs at 0.5 µs). Each source CTA added its nonzero counts into the FFN inboxes before its release, and FFN ranks read them after every arrival. The SM0 control was faster in every H2048 repeat (one token 20.65 vs 21.01 µs, −1.3% to −3.1% up to 512 tokens): moving the prefix sums behind the arrival and completing the remote count atomics before each release cost more than SM0's late release. Not shipped (record).

R9. Shared arrival word (about 1%, inconsistent)​

AFD basis: receivers of the directed exit poll one counter per sender in turn, and an FFN rank starts that wait only after its count wait. FFN ranks send no payload, so a dispatch warp could wait from kernel entry, with all senders adding into one word.

Against its control, the shared word was:

  • 0.5–2% faster at 2A2F;
  • 1–3.5% faster at 3A:1F, consistent at 32 and 128 tokens;
  • mixed at 1A:3F, where one token was slower in 3 of 5 repeats.

In two of the three layouts the repeats disagreed on the winner. It is not shipped (record).

R10. One-CTA receive in one NVLink domain (nothing to free, latency moved)​

The receive-only kernels of one NVLink domain (FFN dispatch, Attention combine) launch the senders' 24-CTA grid, although only CTA 0 waits. A role-sized receive launches CTA 0 alone (the same cubin, one CTA). It passed the GPU matrix, both ratios, the collect view, 7 and 23 transport SMs, every control, mixed-role runs and fault injection: a late sender CTA, a late prefix sum and a late receiver all pass. A receiver counting one arrival per sender returns stale rows, and one expecting an extra arrival times out.

There is little to free: with per-CTA timers, the shared launch's CTAs 1–23 live 0.6 µs and exit, and CTA 0 owns its SM either way. Latency against the shared shape (H100, 2A2F, fresh-process screens):

  • On the candidate digest (a11fe415), one token was slower in 6 of 8 repeats over two pods (3 of 3 on one, median +0.22 µs; 3 of 5 on the other, median +0.04 µs) and eight tokens faster.
  • On scratch builds that only add per-kernel host switches (same device code), one token was slower in 16 of 16 repeats (+0.3–0.4 µs) and eight tokens faster in most.
  • Launched as one cooperative CTA, or as two clustered CTAs, the one-token cost shrank only partly (−0.4% and −0.5% against the one-CTA launch; the shared shape was −1.3% in the same screen).
  • 24 CTAs with the idle warps not launched stayed within ±0.6% of both the in-tree shape and an isolated tree before the role suites at every size except 128 tokens (+1.2–1.3%, two repeats at 3–4%) and 2,048 (+0.1%).

No mechanism was found. An in-process A/B is not possible with this DeepEP build (two Buffers in one process fault with an illegal address, also without the role suites, and two graphs captured on one Buffer corrupt each other), and single-process traces vary more between processes than the effect. So the one-NVLink-domain suites keep the shared launch shapes. What the FFN receive did hold, the copy epilogue, is section 15 (record).
