Sparse directional hybrid

From 1.1.0, when every NVLink domain holds one role, afdEP-comm's directional protocol signals only the channels that carry tokens. It is part of the protocol, not an option: there is nothing to set, and it runs wherever the directional hybrid runs. This page shows what it adds; the design and the correctness argument are in design.md §17.

The cost it removes​

Across NVLink domains, DeepEP's hybrid transport splits every exchange into C channels (192 with 24 SMs), and each channel completes with a signal whether it carried tokens or not. In AFD the traffic flows one way, so these signals converge on the few FFN NICs:

  • in dispatch, each of S Attention domains sends a finish tail on all C channels, S·C RDMA atomics into each FFN NIC (2,112 at 11:1);
  • in combine, the FFN signals every Attention domain on every channel, another S·C out of the same NIC;
  • token i of every source rides channel i mod C, so at small batches every source's first tokens queue on the same FFN forward warp.

At decode batch sizes this synchronization, not the data, sets the round trip: with the dense protocol of 1.0.0, one token per Attention GPU at 11:1 takes 231 µs, and 128 tokens take only twice that.

What it does​

  • Stagger. Source s starts its tokens at channel s·C/S, so the sources' first tokens land on different FFN forward warps, SMs and queue pairs. A channel still carries at most ceil(T/C) tokens of a source.
  • Sparse dispatch. Each source first sends its token count, one word per FFN rail peer. Channels that carried nothing send no finish tail, and FFN forwarders retire them from the count. An FFN NIC takes S·min(T, C) finish atomics plus S count words instead of S·C.
  • Sparse combine. The FFN signals a source only on the channels where it replied to that source, plus channel 0 to every source. Channel 0 is a done witness, sent only after every forward warp has finished reading the exchange, so no source can start the next exchange and overwrite a buffer still in use, even a source that sent nothing.

The three parts change the wire protocol together: construction checks that every rank resolved them alike and rejects a mismatch instead of hanging.

The gain grows with the number of Attention domains, because each one adds C signals to the FFN NICs: −34% at one token with three, −49% with five and −68% with eleven. It holds with two FFN domains, and it fades once the data dominates, from about 128 tokens per Attention GPU. Against FastAFD's DeepEP M2N, one token at 11:1 takes 73.8 µs instead of 339.4 µs (−78%, against an earlier unpaired baseline run), and at 5:1 with hidden 4096, 79.1 µs instead of 205.5 µs on the 1.1.0 tree (−62%, paired in the same screen).

The parts compound. At 11:1 and one token, the stagger alone gains 12% and sparse dispatch alone 34%; together 43%, and sparse combine, worth 2% alone, takes the three to 68%. Once the stagger spreads the replies over many channels, the outbound combine signals become the critical path, and sparse combine removes them (our reading of the timings, not yet traced). What it costs​

  • The done witness. Waiting for every forward warp before channel 0 signals costs 5 µs (+6.6%) at 8 tokens at 5:1 against the earlier, unsafe witness, and is within ±1.2% elsewhere; the round trip there is still 47% below the dense protocol.
  • Large batches gain little. From 128 tokens per Attention GPU the gain falls to 2–9%, and at 256 and 512 tokens to 0–4%. One cell measured slower than dense: +0.9% at 512 tokens with BF16 and hidden 4096 on the 1.1.0 tree, in both repeats; with FP8 at hidden 2048 the same tree measured −0.3% at 512.
  • Per exchange: one count word per source to each FFN rail peer, and the FFN dispatch waits for and clears every count at exit.
  • Code: four generated kernel copies (afdep_source_hybrid_dispatch.cuh, afdep_ffn_sparse_hybrid_dispatch.cuh, afdep_ffn_sparse_hybrid_combine.cuh, afdep_sparse_hybrid_collect.cuh) and one header of protocol words. A protocol bug would show as a hang or wrong values rather than a slowdown, which is why the correctness tests below include faults and a negative control.

Validation​

The GPU suite runs 14 to 16 eager cases, 28 graph replays and 728 checked graph exchanges per run, including empty, inactive and all-skipped sources, skewed routes, delayed replies and dispatches, changing graph inputs and bucket cycles. It passed with the sparse protocol on:

  • 0c22f278 and 200944ce, 24 ranks over 12 × 2 H200: FP8 at capacities 8 and 64, with one and two FFN domains;
  • 2d22e5e9, 6 × 4 H200 (4 FFN + 20 Attention ranks), FP8, capacity 8;
  • 7b8ed8b5, the first tree with the done witness, 6 × 4 H200, FP8, capacity 2,048, with and without forward warps delayed by about 200 µs;
  • 34fa5eac, the shipped cross-node tree, 6 × 4 H200: FP8 and BF16, 4 FFN + 20 Attention and 8 FFN + 16 Attention ranks, capacities 8 and 64, every run passing on all six pods;
  • 21298d5b, the 1.1.0 tree with no switch set, 6 × 4 H200: FP8 and BF16, 4 FFN + 20 Attention and 8 FFN + 16 Attention ranks at capacities 8 and 64, and 4 + 20 at capacity 2,048 with 24 SMs, every run passing on all six pods (832 checked graph exchanges per rank at capacity 8). Both ablation controls set on every rank pass too: AFDEP_SHARED_HYBRID_FORWARD=1 (stagger and sparse combine without sparse dispatch) and AFDEP_SHARED_HYBRID_COLLECT=1 (stagger and sparse dispatch without sparse combine).

As a negative control, a collect that waits only for channel 0 (f92c479c) fails with value mismatches. A shape control set on one role only (for example an FFN-only AFDEP_SHARED_HYBRID_FORWARD=1) is rejected with the agreement error on every rank, on the 1.1.0 tree as before (all six pods report it, none hangs).

Not suite-validated: 8-GPU NVLink domains (measured in section 19's transport screens, not suite-run) and worlds larger than 24 ranks. Record: validation-sparse-hybrid.json.

Comparing with the dense protocol​

There is no switch to turn it off. To measure against the dense protocol, run 1.0.0 as its own arm, as the screens above did: benchmarks/screen.py takes a _tree per arm and checks each arm's native digest. The AFDEP_SHARED_HYBRID_* and AFDEP_SHARED_ROLE_KERNELS ablation controls also turn parts of it off, but they change the kernel shapes as well, so they are not a clean comparison. Set them on every rank, or construction rejects the mismatch.

Why it is an AFD change​

afdEP-comm ships only changes that depend on the AFD roles. This one exists only in the single-role directional protocol, and its correctness depends on each phase flowing one way: Attention domains receive only combine signals, and FFN domains receive only dispatch traffic. The S:1 ratio of Attention to FFN domains is what concentrates the signals on the FFN NICs. Signalling only used channels might also help DeepEP's own union hybrid at small batches; that is neither implemented nor measured here.
